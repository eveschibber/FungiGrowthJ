Overview

1. Introduction

FungiGrowthJ is an open workflow for Fiji/ImageJ designed to organize, validate, measure, and analyze longitudinal fungal colony growth experiments on Petri dishes.

The tool was designed for photographs in which the outer colony boundaries were manually outlined with a black marker. It can quantify the central colony and satellite colonies, preserve review decisions, and export calibrated results in physical units.

Objective: automate repetitive work without hiding scientific decisions. Every relevant step is shown, validated, and recorded.

Final status

  • Experiment Manager with structure and manifest validation.
  • Integrated launcher and reproducible Batch workflow.
  • Complete single-image wizard.
  • Spatial calibration and controlled reuse of agar references.
  • Marker mask using IJ_IsoData with morphological closing and optional gap closure.
  • Manual removal of dark artifacts from the detection mask without modifying the original photograph.
  • Enhanced partitioning of connected objects and manual seed refinement when needed.
  • Colony detection, measurement, review, classification, and editing.
  • Persistence of automatic and final ROIs for traceability.
  • CSV export with scientific results, parameters, and metadata.
  • QA Workflow and persistent Batch states: PENDING , IN_PROGRESS , REVIEW , ERROR , COMPLETED , among others.
Final release: this version documents the FungiGrowthJ behavior used for publication. No further functional changes are proposed within this release.
Design philosophy

2. Design principles

Transparency

No automatic decision that may affect a scientific result should remain hidden.

Untouched originals

Original images are never modified.

Human review

The software proposes; the user confirms, corrects, or ignores.

Reproducibility

Versions, parameters, calibration, states, and actions are recorded.

Knowledge built into the software

The wizard explains what to do, why to do it, and how to evaluate the result.

Modular architecture

Components are separated into management, segmentation, review, measurement, and export.

Quick start

3. Quick start

  1. Organize images using the canonical experiment structure.
  2. Run FGJ_ExperimentManager_v0_4 .
  3. Select the folder that directly contains the strain folders.
  4. Review the plate tables and QA issues.
  5. Export the experiment manifest.
  6. Open an image and run FGJ_SingleImageWorkflow_v0_3 .
  7. Complete the six wizard steps.
  8. Save the reviewed CSV.
In the current version, Experiment Manager and image analysis are two separate workflows. Release 0.5 will integrate them into a single launcher.

Conventions

  • Dates must strictly use YYYYMMDD .
  • Keeping the original camera filename is recommended.
  • Strain and replicate names may be arbitrary, but they must be consistent.
  • There must be exactly one image per date folder.
  • Empty folders are valid only if they are documented as observations without an image; they currently generate a QA alert.
Selecting the correct folder is essential. Experiment Manager expects strain folders to be directly inside the selected folder.

Single image workflow

6. Single-image wizard

The current workflow contains six steps: 

[#-----] Step 1 of 6 — Set image scale
[##----] Step 2 of 6 — Select agar sample
[###---] Step 3 of 6 — Detect agar region
[####--] Step 4 of 6 — Detect colonies
[#####-] Step 5 of 6 — Review objects
[######] Step 6 of 6 — Export results

If the user presses Next without making the required selection, the wizard shows Try again and remains on the same step.

Step 1

7. Set the image scale

Action

  1. Choose the tool Straight Line .
  2. Draw a line over a known distance.
  3. Enter the value and unit.

Recommendation

Use 10 or 20 mm instead of 1 mm to reduce positioning error.

Calculations 

pixels_per_unit = line_length_px / known_distance
unit_per_pixel = known_distance / line_length_px

QA checks

  • the selection must be a line;
  • the distance must be greater than zero;
  • the line should not be too short;
  • pixel measurements must be calculated before calibration;
  • conversion to millimeters must be controlled.
Step 2

8. Create an agar sample

The user draws a small ROI over clean, representative agar.

Avoid

  • colonies;
  • black marker;
  • bubbles;
  • strong reflections;
  • areas with extreme illumination.

The sample serves two purposes:

  1. provides the average RGB color of the agar;
  2. provides a seed point that belongs to the agar.
Add screenshot: example of correct and incorrect sample
Step 3

9. Detect the agar

Detection is interactive and updates in real time.

Parameter Function When to modify it
RGB color tolerance Accepts colors similar to the sample. Increase if agar is missing; reduce if background is included.
Median radius Reduces local noise. Use low values.
Closing iterations Connects fragmented regions. Increase if agar remains fragmented.
Opening iterations Removes small isolated regions. Use conservatively.
Circle offset Expands or shrinks the ROI. Negative shrinks; positive expands.

Algorithm 

Sample ROI
↓
Mean RGB
↓
RGB color-distance mask
↓
Median + closing + opening
↓
Connected component containing the seed
↓
Fit Circle
↓
Signed circle offset
↓
Confirmed agar ROI

Acceptance criterion

The ROI should follow the usable agar region, not the outer glass edge.

Step 4

10. Generate the marker mask

Within the confirmed agar ROI, FungiGrowthJ operates on the brightness component and applies IJ_IsoData to separate dark structures from the background. 

RGB image
↓
HSB brightness
↓
Histogram inside agar ROI
↓
IJ_IsoData
↓
Dark-marker mask
↓
Binary closing
↓
Optional marker gap closure
↓
Fill holes

The workflow was designed to preserve marker-drawn contours and reduce false positives associated with broad illumination variation.

Marker gap closure

Marker gap closure iterations bridges small interruptions in the dark trace before the fill stage. The default value is 0 . It should only be increased when the real contour contains short breaks that prevent a closed region from being formed.

Do not confuse this with general closing: marker gap closure is a conservative, contour-specific correction for small breaks. It should not replace proper mask review.

Manual cleaning of dark artifacts

When the image contains dots, stains, or other dark structures that are not part of the marker, you can enable Clean dark artifacts manually before detection .

  1. FungiGrowthJ displays the binary mask used for detection.
  2. The Paintbrush with black to remove from the mask the white regions corresponding to artifacts.
  3. The original photograph remains intact.
  4. The cleaned mask is immediately used in the subsequent detection stages.

This feature is intended for difficult images and is not necessary for clean photographs.

Best practices: erase only clearly identifiable artifacts. Do not erase a region just because it has an irregular shape: a real colony may be irregular.

Manual agar-zone mask

Mask agar zones manually before detection allows specific agar sectors to be excluded from detection without altering the photograph.

Black letters and annotations may appear in the mask. FungiGrowthJ must not automatically assume that they are colonies: they are reviewed and, when appropriate, classified as IGN .
Step 4

11. Detect and measure objects

Fiji/ImageJ converts closed contours into measurable objects. Normal detection uses a minimum candidate area and may exclude objects that touch the agar ROI boundary.

Initial measurements

  • area;
  • perimeter;
  • centroid;
  • circularity;
  • distance to agar center.

Connected-colony partition

Enhanced touching-colony partition allows objects that remained connected after segmentation to be separated. The method searches for closed interior structures within a connected object and generates independent ROIs when the geometric evidence is sufficient.

The parameter Minimum enclosed interior area controls the minimum size of these interior regions. Its default value is 150 px² .

Manual seed refinement

When a connected colony cannot be separated correctly by automatic partitioning, a manual seed can be used. Seeds refine only the corresponding connected object; all other proposals remain unchanged.

Very small candidates

The option Detect very small / point-like colony candidates enables a second pass for small objects. These candidates are considered low-confidence and are sent for explicit review; they are never silently accepted.

Automatic classification

  • the object closest to the agar center is proposed as the central colony;
  • the remaining objects are proposed as satellites;
  • the proposal never replaces human confirmation.

Alerts

Code Meaning
LOW_CIRC Low circularity.
LOW_CIRC_EDIT Low circularity after editing.
NEAR_EDGE Object near the agar edge.
AREA_OUTLIER Atypical area.
Step 5

12. Review and edit colonies

Objects are shown from lowest to highest circularity so that suspicious cases are reviewed first. The color of an ROI primarily indicates its visual review state; green means visually accepted; it does not automatically mean "central" .

Final types

Code Meaning
CEN Central colony.
SAT Satellite colony.
IGN Ignore.
DEF Deferred review.
The central colony must be explicitly confirmed. The central/satellite classification used in downstream calculations corresponds to the decision confirmed during review, not simply to the visual position on the plate.

Edit an ROI

  1. Modify the selected ROI using Fiji tools.
  2. Presionar Update in ROI Manager.
  3. Mark that the ROI was edited.
  4. Recalculate and confirm.

Split a merged ROI

If an ROI contains two or more colonies, use Split ROI into multiple colonies . First specify how many colonies are present, then draw each child ROI, and finally confirm the complete set. Child ROIs retain traceability to the original object.

Early completion

The user can preview the remaining objects. Unreviewed objects appear with ? . The user can then continue, jump to a specific ID, or accept the remaining proposals.

Ignored objects are preserved by default in ROI Manager for auditability, even though they do not appear in the scientific output.

Temporal workflow

14. Dates, absences, and growth

The identity of each observation comes from the folder structure: strain, replicate, and date.

Future observation states

State Area Interpretation
measured Measured from image. Image exists.
no_growth_detected 0 if there was no previous growth. The plate was observed and there was no growth.
no_change_observed Carries forward the previous value. An observation occurred, but growth was zero.
missing NA. It is unknown what happened.
harvested End of follow-up. The colony reached the edge and was harvested.
excluded NA. Observation excluded.
Absence of an image must never be automatically interpreted as zero growth.
Quality assurance

15. Quality Assurance Workflow

FungiGrowthJ separates observation state from scientific outcome. An observation may require review even when a valid image exists, and an observation in ERROR must not be considered analyzed.

Scientific and workflow states 

PENDING
IN_PROGRESS

ERROR
COMPLETED

INTERRUPTED

State Practical interpretation
PENDING Not yet processed.
IN_PROGRESS Processing in progress.
REVIEW Requires review or a pending human decision.
ERROR Processing did not finish validly. The result must not be assumed to be complete.
COMPLETED The observation was closed as a complete analysis.
SKIPPED Intentionally excluded from processing.
INTERRUPTED Interrupted during processing and available to resume.
TRAVERSED Image traversed/validated by the workflow, but not by itself equivalent to a complete scientific analysis.

Checkpoints

  1. Experiment structure.
  2. Experiment summary.
  3. Image compatibility.
  4. Calibration.
  5. Agar ROI.
  6. Marker mask.
  7. Object detection.
  8. Human review.
  9. Measurement validation.
  10. Export.

Batch: review images with REVIEW or ERROR status

The final Batch workflow allows visual review of observations that remain in REVIEW or ERROR . The user can open the image, inspect the saved ROIs, and explicitly decide whether the observation should be set to COMPLETED or left in its current state.

This review action does not imply automatic reanalysis . It must be distinguished from rerunning the analysis.

Important: An observation that has already been reviewed should not be analyzed again just to change its state. First verify whether persisted results and ROIs exist or use Batch visual review.

Troubleshooting

16. Common errors

Problem Likely cause Solution
Black or nearly empty mask Marker intensity was not adequately represented by the automatic threshold. Inspect the mask and, if appropriate, adjust the detection context or image preparation.
Many unwanted dark dots The photograph contains dirt, stains, or dark structures that also pass the threshold. Activar Clean dark artifacts manually before detection and erase only the obvious artifacts from the mask.
The marker has small breaks The trace does not form closed regions. Try Marker gap closure iterations with a low value, for example 1, and review the result.
A large region contains multiple colonies Colonies or their contours remained connected. Activar Enhanced touching-colony partition ; if this is still insufficient, use manual seeds or Split ROI .
Very small objects are detected They may be small colonies or artifacts. Utilizar Detect very small / point-like colony candidates only when necessary and review those objects explicitly.
Letters or annotations are detected They are dark structures that are valid for segmentation. Review them as objects and classify them as IGN when they are not colonies.
The proposed central colony is not correct The automatic proposal is based on position relative to the agar center. During review, explicitly select central for the correct ROI.
An ROI contains multiple colonies Object merging during segmentation. Use Enhanced touching-colony partition , manual seeds or Split ROI .
Batch shows ERROR Processing did not reach a completed state. Do not assume that the result is closed. Review the observation visually or reanalyze it only if truly necessary.
Batch leaves an observation in REVIEW The observation requires a human decision. Use the Batch review function and explicitly decide COMPLETED or leave it in review.
General rule: when an image requires manual intervention, that intervention must be documented in review and must not alter the original photograph.

Reproducibility

20. Reproducibility and publication

The public release should distribute the software and documentation as a coherent set.

  • final-release launcher and modules;
  • updated user manual;
  • functional specification and architecture;
  • QA Workflow;
  • example experiment;
  • expected results or reference data when applicable;
  • README;
  • CHANGELOG;
  • MODULE_REGISTRY;
  • LICENSE;
  • CITATION.cff;
  • CONTRIBUTING.md;
  • biological analysis notebook separate from the measurement software.
The separation between measurement and biological analysis is part of the reproducible design: FungiGrowthJ generates measurements and traceability; the notebook performs subsequent summaries, statistical analyses, and figures.
